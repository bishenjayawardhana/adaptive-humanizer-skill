In today's rapidly evolving artificial intelligence landscape, serving long-context large language models plays a pivotal role in enabling enterprise reasoning applications. However, the memory footprint of the key-value (KV) cache poses a formidable bottleneck during autoregressive decoding, thereby underscoring the urgent need for scalable, efficient, and robust compression strategies. This study delves into the intricate trade-offs between numerical precision, memory bandwidth, and generation fidelity when applying mixed-precision quantization to transformer attention caches.

Furthermore, our empirical evaluation on Llama-3-8B running on a single A100-80GB GPU showcases that asymmetric INT4 quantization reduces KV-cache VRAM consumption by 38.4% at a 16k context window compared to an FP16 baseline. Moreover, this memory savings translates into a 1.72x improvement in batch decoding throughput, effectively demonstrating the practical viability of low-bit caching under heavy serving loads. It is important to note that naive uniform quantization degrades output quality sharply on retrieval-heavy prompts; consequently, retaining the initial four sink tokens and the most recent 128 window tokens in FP16 limits WikiText-2 perplexity degradation to just 0.41 points.

Additionally, prior work by Dettmers et al. (2022) and Hooper et al. (2024) established that activation outliers are not merely random noise, but rather a structural cornerstone of transformer attention routing. Notably, our channel-wise scaling analysis confirms this multifaceted behavior across all 32 decoder layers—highlighting why per-token calibration outperforms per-tensor schemes in long-document summarization. Ultimately, these findings serve as a testament to the transformative potential of hardware-aware cache quantization, paving the way for faster, cheaper, and more accessible long-context inference.
